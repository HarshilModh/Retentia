# Meiosis: Sexual Reproduction and Genetic Diversity

Meiosis is a specialized form of cell division that reduces the chromosome number by half, resulting in the production of four genetically diverse haploid gametes (sperm or egg cells). It is essential for sexual reproduction.

## The Two Stages of Meiosis
Meiosis consists of two successive cell divisions, Meiosis I and Meiosis II:

### Meiosis I: Homologous Separation
1. **Prophase I**: Chromosomes condense, and homologous chromosomes pair up in a process called synapsis. Crucially, chromatids cross over and exchange genetic material at junctions called chiasmata. This creates unique combinations of maternal and paternal genes.
2. **Metaphase I**: Homologous chromosome pairs align at the center of the cell.
3. **Anaphase I**: Homologous chromosomes are pulled to opposite poles (sister chromatids remain attached).
4. **Telophase I & Cytokinesis**: Two haploid daughter cells are formed, each containing one chromosome from each homologous pair.

### Meiosis II: Sister Chromatid Separation
Meiosis II is very similar to mitosis:
1. **Prophase II**: Chromosomes condense again in both daughter cells.
2. **Metaphase II**: Chromosomes align individually at the metaphase plate.
3. **Anaphase II**: Sister chromatids are pulled apart to opposite poles.
4. **Telophase II & Cytokinesis**: The nuclei reform, and division completes.

## Genetic Diversity Outcomes
Meiosis generates genetic variation through two mechanisms:
- **Crossing Over (Prophase I)**: Exchanges DNA segments between homologous chromosomes.
- **Independent Assortment (Metaphase I)**: Random orientation of homologous pairs at the cell equator.

Meiosis results in four haploid gametes, each genetically distinct from the parent cell and from each other.
